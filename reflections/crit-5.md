# Reflection

1. I felt that the breakthrough for this game actually came from the ideation stage.
I wanted to build a novel game, drawing on inspiration from other games that I enjoy; and ultimately, I wanted to build something that I will enjoy playing myself.
Personally, I like playing Wordle-like games and puzzles everyday because they don't take up too much time, but are still fun and rewarding.
On the other hand, I don't enjoy traditional video games as much anymore as they are too time-consuming and simply just not appealing.
I thought I would try asking Claude using my personal account for game ideas, but none of its ideas really stood out to me; some were too complex and some were simply not novel.
I paused ideation with Claude, and went about my day.
I met with my girlfriend, but I arrived a bit late.
She told me I need to have better planning skills, and that's when it struck me, I wanted to build a task planning game.
I discussed the idea with her, and we both felt like this was a novel idea, and something we'd actually play.
I then looped back to my previous ideas, especially the ones with Wordle, and felt incorporating the daily puzzle element would increase long-term appeal.

2. Even though agentic coding rapidly speeds up the development process, I realise that ideation definitely still needs a human in the loop.
A human driving an agent might not even be enough to generate ideas, but it is the lived experiences and creativity of a human that is still necessary to generate ideas.
These inputs ensure that the output is something that will appeal and be useful to others, and that is one of key objectives of building software.
